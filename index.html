Working memory

How many digits can you hold in mind? Then try again while your mouth is busy.

What you'll do

  1. Digits appear one at a time.
  2. When they stop, type them in order and press Enter.
  3. The lists get longer until you miss two in a row at the same length.
  4. Round 2 is the same, but you say "the, the, the" out loud the whole time.

About 5 minutes for both rounds. No writing the digits down or tapping them out!

Your results appear here after both rounds.

What's going on (open after you finish)

Working memory is a small, short-term workspace that holds and works with information for a few seconds. George Miller (1956) put its span at about "seven, plus or minus two" items.

One part of working memory, the phonological loop, keeps words alive by silently repeating them with your inner voice. Saying "the, the, the" ties up your speech system, so the inner voice can't rehearse. This is called articulatory suppression, and it usually lowers span by one or two digits.

Language needs this workspace all the time. To understand The dog that the cat chased ran away, you have to hold the dog until ran arrives. While speaking, you plan the next words while still saying the current ones. When working memory is overloaded, people produce simpler sentences, pause more, and make more agreement errors.
